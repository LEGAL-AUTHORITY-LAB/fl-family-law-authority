# Rule 12.351 — Nonparty Document Subpoena Guide

**Rule**: Fla. Fam. L.R.P. 12.351  
**Critical**: This is the EXCLUSIVE procedure for obtaining documents or things by subpoena from nonparties without deposing the custodian or other person in possession.  
Do NOT issue a nonparty document subpoena under any other rule.

---

## BEFORE DRAFTING A NONPARTY SUBPOENA — CHECKLIST

- [ ] Is this source truly a nonparty? (Not a party, not a party's alter ego business)
- [ ] Is this information available from the opposing party first? (Try party discovery first when the party has or controls the records)
- [ ] Is the scope of records requested proportional to the issues at stake?
- [ ] Are there privacy or privilege issues? (Medical, mental health, therapy records — these likely require separate authorization or court order)
- [ ] Has attorney reviewed and approved the subpoena?
- [ ] Is proper notice being given to all parties?
- [ ] Is the return location and date in the proper county?

---

## COMMON NONPARTY SUBPOENA TARGETS IN FAMILY CASES

| Target | What to Request |
|---|---|
| Banks / credit unions | Account statements, signature cards, loan documents for specific accounts and date ranges |
| Brokerage / investment firms | Account statements, trade confirmations, transfer records |
| Employers | Payroll records, W-2s, offer letters, compensation records, bonus history |
| Accountants / CPAs | Tax preparation files, work papers, communications, financial statements prepared for client |
| Business entities (if not party) | Financial statements, payroll, bank records, ownership records |
| Payroll processors (ADP, Paychex) | Payroll reports, compensation data |
| Venmo / PayPal / Cash App / Zelle | Transaction history for specific date range |
| Crypto exchanges (Coinbase, etc.) | Account statements, transaction records |
| DCF | Records relating to minor children (Florida Public Records / Chapter 39 records) |
| Schools | Attendance records, enrollment records, communications with parents |
| Healthcare providers | Records of minor child (requires careful handling — not the parent's records without authorization) |
| Real estate records | Title searches are usually public record — check county property appraiser first |

---

## PRIVACY FLAGS — DO NOT SUBPOENA WITHOUT ATTORNEY CLEARANCE

**Mental health records**: Florida statute §394.4615 and §491.0147 provide strong confidentiality protections. A court order or patient authorization is typically required. Do not serve a subpoena for therapy records without specific attorney direction.

**Substance abuse records**: 42 C.F.R. Part 2 provides federal protections for substance abuse treatment records. Court order required.

**HIPAA / Medical records**: An authorization or court order is typically required for the opposing party's medical records. Minor child's records may require both parents' authorization or a court order.

**DCF records**: Chapter 39 creates confidentiality protections. Petition to the court for access is the correct route, not a direct subpoena.

**Financial records of third parties**: Proportionality matters. Be precise about accounts, date ranges, and the issue you are trying to prove.

```
IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF THE [JUDICIAL CIRCUIT] CIRCUIT,
IN AND FOR [COUNTY] COUNTY, FLORIDA

IN RE: THE MATTER OF:                    CASE NO.: [CASE NUMBER]
[PETITIONER] vs. [RESPONDENT]
_________________________________/

SUBPOENA FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS AND THINGS
(Pursuant to Fla. Fam. L.R.P. 12.351)
(Non-Deposition)

TO: [RECORDS CUSTODIAN NAME]
    [INSTITUTION NAME]
    [ADDRESS]

YOU ARE COMMANDED to produce and permit inspection and copying of the following 
documents and things at the offices of FAMILY MATTERS LAW GROUP, P.A., [ADDRESS], 
[COUNTY] County, Florida, on or before [DATE], or as otherwise agreed:

DOCUMENTS REQUESTED:

1. All account statements for account number(s) [SPECIFY / "all accounts held in 
the name of [PARTY NAME]"] for the period [DATE] through [DATE].

2. [CONTINUE WITH SPECIFIC REQUESTS — be precise about accounts, date ranges, types 
of records. Do not use "all records" without a scope limiter.]

NOTICE TO PARTY:
Copies of this subpoena have been served on all parties of record. Any party may 
object to production of any of the specified items within the time provided under 
Rule 12.351.

Pursuant to Fla. Fam. L.R.P. 12.351, [RECORDS HOLDER] is not required to produce 
the requested documents in the presence of any party or counsel unless so ordered 
by the court. Production may be made by mailing or delivering the records directly 
to the address above.

This subpoena is issued pursuant to Florida Family Law Rule of Procedure 12.351 
and is not a deposition subpoena.

Issued by:
[ATTORNEY NAME], Esquire
Florida Bar No.: [BAR NUMBER]
FAMILY MATTERS LAW GROUP, P.A.
[ADDRESS] | [PHONE] | [EMAIL]
Attorney for [PARTY]

CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE
[Certify service on all parties — NOT just the subpoena target]
```

---

## NOTICE REQUIREMENTS — Rule 12.351

- Notice of the subpoena and a copy must be served on all parties at the same time the subpoena is served on the nonparty
- Parties have the right to object to the production
- Check local rules for any additional notice period requirements

# Incoming Discovery Response Review Framework

Use this framework when the other side has responded to discovery you served. Work through each response systematically.

---

## RESPONSE REVIEW CHECKLIST

For each request / interrogatory / RFA:

**1. Was a response served at all?**
- If no response: Note date discovery was served, date response was due, and that no response was received. Confirm whether an extension was agreed to. Begin deficiency letter process.

**2. Was the response timely?**
- RFP / Interrogatories: Due within 30 days of service
- RFAs: Due within 30 days — failure = DEEMED ADMITTED
- If untimely: Note whether objections are therefore waived (they generally are if no timely response)

**3. For each request — categorize the response:**

| Response Type | Analysis |
|---|---|
| Full production / full answer | Confirm completeness against what you know about the case |
| Partial production with no explanation | Flag missing portion; note deficiency |
| Objection only, no production | Analyze objection — does it have merit? Is it boilerplate? |
| Objection + partial production | Is the non-objectionable portion fully produced? |
| "Documents will be produced" with no actual production | Note the representation; if no production follows, it is a deficiency |
| Verified answers (interrogatories) | Confirm answer is actually under oath / notarized |

**4. Analyze each objection:**

| Objection | Analysis |
|---|---|
| Overbroad | Is the request actually overbroad? If targeted, no basis. Does the objection say WHY it's overbroad? |
| Unduly burdensome | Any factual support for burden claim? Usually no. |
| Not reasonably calculated... | Outdated standard under Florida rules. Note this. |
| Vague and ambiguous | Does the response then answer the "vague" request anyway? Contradicts itself. |
| Privileged | Proper? Is a privilege log required? |
| Not in possession / custody / control | Is this credible? Narrower request may be needed, or interrogatory to identify who holds the records. |

**5. For financial records — cross-check against what you know:**
- FA discloses account at Bank X → Did bank statements for that account get produced?
- Tax return shows Schedule C income → Did business records get produced?
- Party claims one employer → Did complete pay stubs get produced?
- Any account disclosed on FA that has NO corresponding statements = deficiency

**6. Output: Deficiency list for deficiency letter**

For each deficiency, note:
- Request number
- What was requested
- What was produced (if anything)
- What is specifically missing
- The legal basis for requiring production
- Whether a narrower request would resolve it
