Jurisdiction and Venue in Florida Family Law

By Leisa Wintz, Esq. | Family Matters Law Group, P.A.

"Jurisdiction" and "venue" are often used interchangeably but mean different things — and getting them confused can cause real delays in a family law case. Jurisdiction is whether a court has the legal authority to hear your case at all; venue is which specific county is the proper place to file it.

Venue Rules by Case Type

Florida sets specific venue rules depending on the type of family law case:

  • Divorce — generally filed in the county where the couple last lived together as a married couple.
  • Paternity — can typically be filed in the county where the mother, father, or child resides.
  • Name change — must be filed in the county where the petitioner currently lives; this requirement is non-waivable.
  • Partition action (dividing jointly owned property between unmarried parties) — filed in the county where the property is located.

Venue Can Usually Be Waived

In most family law cases, venue is waivable — meaning if the other party doesn't object to where the case was filed, it simply proceeds in that county. If a case is filed somewhere that isn't proper, the other party can file a motion for change of venue, but they have to raise the objection; the court won't automatically catch it.

Why Venue Choice Matters Strategically

Where a case is filed can matter for practical reasons beyond strict legal requirements — convenience, local judicial procedures, or even privacy considerations. Some parties choose to file in a different eligible county for these reasons, provided the choice complies with the applicable venue rule and local court procedures.
